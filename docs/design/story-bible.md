# Echo Ridge — story bible

**Status:** Narrative direction approved. The first story-mode foundation is integrated on main: persistent campaign progression, a skippable nine-beat prologue presentation, radio/subtitle dialogue, evidence collection and progression through the three existing mission scenes. The prologue is not yet a playable 3D sequence; later missions, the full boss encounters and ending presentation remain unbuilt.
**Genre:** Cinematic tactical action thriller / near-future corporate noir.
**Setting:** 2041, in the fictional Meridian Corridor. Rural mountain passes, industrial towns and a neon-lit corporate city sit beneath a dense private surveillance network.
**Design promise:** A personal revenge story with the escalating scale, hard-edged action and memorable set pieces of a modern action film, combined with original cyberpunk worldbuilding. This is an original story; it does not use John Wick or Cyberpunk characters, plots, dialogue or settings.

## The hook

They took his home, killed the person who brought him back from the war, and made the world believe he was the monster.

Now the most powerful security corporation in the Meridian Corridor has a problem: the man it tried to erase is still alive.

## The protagonist

**Elias Vance — callsign: Echo**

Elias is a former special reconnaissance marksman who spent years doing deniable work for governments and private clients. He left that life after a mission went wrong and built a quiet life with his wife, **Lena Vale**. He is patient, observant and frighteningly capable, but he is not invincible. He has old injuries, limited resources and a conscience that makes some choices harder than pulling a trigger.

Elias is not a superhero with endless ammunition. He survives through preparation, positioning, weapon discipline, reading people and adapting when plans collapse. His reputation is a threat to the corporation because he knows how its operators think.

## The inciting tragedy

Lena was a systems engineer who discovered that **Asterion Continuity**, a powerful private-security and infrastructure corporation, was secretly using its predictive surveillance platform to identify, disappear and replace people who threatened its contracts. Its field arm, the **Varn Accord**, carried out the work.

Lena copied evidence into a fragmented archive called the **Black Ledger**. Before she could expose it, an Accord strike team attacked the couple's remote home. The house was destroyed, Lena was killed, and Elias was left gravely wounded. Asterion then altered the available surveillance record and framed Elias as the attacker.

The opening is intimate and quiet before it becomes violent. The loss is not used as a disposable shock: Lena's choices, recordings and work continue to shape the story. The player learns who she was through memories, personal objects and the evidence she left behind.

## The conspiracy

**Asterion Continuity** sells security, logistics, predictive analytics and autonomous defense systems to cities and private clients. Its public image is clean and reassuring. Its real business is control.

- **Varn Accord:** Asterion's deniable private military arm. Professional, disciplined and increasingly augmented with networked optics, smart targeting and drone support.
- **NIGHTGLASS:** The illegal surveillance and identity-manipulation program Lena uncovered. It combines city cameras, compromised personal devices, biometric records and predictive software to manufacture threats before they happen.
- **The Black Ledger:** Lena's fragmented proof: payment trails, mission orders, erased identities and the names of people who approved NIGHTGLASS.
- **High Cairn:** Asterion's remote command and uplink facility. It is the physical heart of the final operation and the place where the corporation's director intends to erase the last evidence.

Cybernetic technology is present but grounded: smart optics, drone swarms, biometric locks, surveillance overlays, signal jammers, remote turrets and experimental combat interfaces. The player uses practical weapons and fieldcraft against an enemy that can see too much.

## Principal cast

### Elias Vance / Echo — protagonist
A disciplined former marksman whose personal loss turns into a fight against the system that ordered it. His arc is not about becoming a better killer; it is about deciding what kind of man survives the revenge.

### Lena Vale — the person he lost
Engineer, whistleblower and Elias's wife. Her voice appears through recorded messages and recovered fragments. She was brave, funny and stubborn, not simply a plot device. Her archive is the reason the campaign exists.

### Mara Quill — handler and former range officer
A trusted voice from Elias's old life. She helps him interpret the evidence and find safe routes. Mara has withheld part of the truth: she knew Lena was investigating Asterion and failed to protect her. She is culpable through fear and compromise, not secretly the mastermind. The relationship must be allowed to become difficult.

### Imani Rook — underground systems broker
A resourceful fixer who can decode pieces of the Black Ledger and help Elias move through monitored territory. She is practical, skeptical and has her own reasons to hate Asterion. She provides access and perspective, not magic hacking that solves every problem.

### Director Silas Vey — primary antagonist
Founder and director of Asterion Continuity. Vey is a former intelligence architect who believes privacy is an obsolete luxury and that stability justifies any price. He does not see himself as evil; he sees people as risks to be managed. He ordered the strike on Elias and Lena because the Black Ledger threatened his control of NIGHTGLASS.

Vey is calm, precise and personally involved. He should appear throughout the campaign in briefings, broadcasts and private recordings so the final confrontation is with a character the player knows—not a stranger introduced at the end.

### Commander Rusk — field antagonist
The Accord commander who led the raid. A professional who treats violence as procedure. He is the recurring face of Asterion's force and the first major personal target. His presence ties the opening tragedy to the larger corporate conspiracy.

## Campaign structure

The campaign has a cinematic prologue/tutorial followed by eight main missions. Each mission advances the mystery, changes the balance of power and introduces a distinct gameplay challenge.

| Chapter | Mission | Story beat | Gameplay/set piece |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | The Last Quiet Night | Establish Elias and Lena's life, then the attack, injury and framing. Elias escapes with a damaged fragment of the Black Ledger. | Short playable domestic exploration, shooting-range tutorial, sudden ambush and escape. |
| 1 | Kestrel Pass | Elias tracks the Accord unit that attacked his home and discovers the operation was ordered, not improvised. | Long-range overwatch, wind, ridge routes, outpost infiltration. |
| 2 | Echo Ridge | Recover the first complete ledger shard and learn the name NIGHTGLASS. | Intel recovery, extraction under pressure, surveillance searchlights and drone patrols. |
| 3 | The Compound | Break into an Accord logistics site and identify Commander Rusk. | Close stealth, body hiding, silent objectives and multiple entry routes. |
| 4 | Lowwater Bridge | Intercept a convoy carrying a witness and a mobile command server before both disappear. | Moving vehicle target, timed convoy, alternate non-lethal witness rescue route. |
| 5 | Glass Hollow | Enter an Asterion research facility and discover the experimental systems used by Accord operators. | Night infiltration, thermal optics, power control and enemy smart optics. |
| 6 | Saltmarsh Station | Asterion deploys a counter-sniper to eliminate Elias and his allies. | Cat-and-mouse sniper duel, decoys, changing sightlines and limited visibility. |
| 7 | Overwatch | Protect Imani and a small group of defectors while they transmit the Black Ledger. Elias learns Vey is preparing to erase the evidence at High Cairn. | Protect a moving ally team, changing positions, coordinated threats and a hard extraction. |
| 8 | High Cairn — The Last Contract | Elias reaches Asterion's uplink facility, confronts Rusk and then Vey, and decides what to do with the evidence after the fight. | Multi-stage infiltration, disabling the network, close-quarters boss encounter and cinematic ending. |

## Final mission and boss encounter

The finale must be a real change of pace from a normal sniper mission.

1. **The approach:** Elias uses the terrain and the facility's blind spots to reach High Cairn. The player can enter quietly or trigger a full lockdown.
2. **The shutdown:** The player disables three parts of the uplink network. Each one changes enemy behavior, security coverage or the available route.
3. **The reckoning:** Commander Rusk confronts Elias. This is a personal fight with a familiar enemy, not a generic boss health bar.
4. **The director:** Silas Vey is encountered in the control core. He uses the facility, remote security and an experimental targeting interface to stay dangerous. The fight moves between cover, close-range weapon work and moments where the player must outthink the system.
5. **The choice:** Elias secures the Black Ledger. The ending choice is whether to release the full archive publicly, hand it to an independent inquiry through Imani, or destroy the operational data while preserving the names of the victims. Vey's defeat is the climax; the choice determines what Elias does with the truth.

The boss must be challenging because of readable phases and changing tactics—not inflated health. Telegraph attacks, give the player cover and counterplay, and never make the outcome depend on an unavoidable hit.

## Tone, presentation and player agency

- Cinematic, grounded and intense; violence has consequences and is not played as comedy.
- Near-future technology should feel plausible and functional, not magical.
- Use quiet scenes, environmental storytelling and restrained dialogue to make the action matter.
- Keep Lena present through her recorded voice, notes and the impact of her decisions, without turning every mission into a flashback.
- The player may choose stealth, long-range engagement or direct action where the level supports it. Choices can change routes, alert levels, dialogue and mission results.
- Do not punish players with a completely different story for playing loudly. Preserve the central story while acknowledging how the player approached it.
- Keep civilians out of forced kill scenarios. Provide clear identification and meaningful alternatives where possible.
- Fictional places, organizations, weapons and companies only.

## Storytelling systems to build

1. Cinematic prologue and mission-opening sequences with skippable playback.
2. Dialogue/subtitle system with speaker names, timing, pause/resume and localization.
3. Radio conversations that can react to stealth, alarms, optional objectives and prior mission outcomes.
4. Collectible evidence viewer for Black Ledger fragments, with a clear chronology and no mandatory scavenger hunt for core plot facts.
5. Character dossier and mission debrief pages.
6. Story flags saved with campaign progress, including major evidence and ending choice.
7. Mission transitions, chapter cards and a final ending sequence.
8. Boss encounter framework with phase changes, telegraphs, cover and checkpoint support.

## Production order

- **Narrative lock:** approve the premise, cast, tragedy, antagonist and ending direction.
- **Script pass:** write the prologue, mission briefings, radio beats, evidence fragments and boss dialogue.
- **Story integration:** connect existing mission objectives and mission results to narrative flags.
- **Cinematic implementation:** build reusable camera, subtitle, dialogue and transition tools.
- **Vertical slice:** implement the prologue through Kestrel Pass with temporary voice/text and placeholder cinematics.
- **Full campaign:** expand chapter by chapter; each mission must be playable and tested before its story item is marked complete.
- **Polish:** voice performance, music, sound design, facial animation, localization and Windows playtesting.

## Current implementation status

This document is a narrative blueprint. The existing mission prototypes and systems are not yet a fully scripted story campaign. No cinematic scenes, recorded dialogue, boss fight or ending choice should be described as implemented until they exist in the game and pass their verification steps.
